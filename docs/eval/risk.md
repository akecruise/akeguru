## Manual review (checkBulletGrounding can't check these)

**1. Is `example` a genuinely concrete scenario, or "For example," + a restated `body`?** — read through by hand, all 5 items (3 MSFT + 2 1773.HK) pass: every `example` states a real conditional ("if AI infra demand slows while CapEx stays at $115.9B...", "if lenders refuse to roll over short-term debt...") that adds a *consequence*, not a repeat of the claim in `body`.

**2. Do two risk items in the same run cover the same underlying risk under different names?** — MSFT's 3 items are cleanly distinct (CapEx/FCF compression, valuation de-rating, debt-vs-cash). 1773.HK's 2 items are *related but not duplicates*: "short-term liquidity" (Current Ratio + Cash vs Total Debt) and "leverage vs FCF" (Debt/Equity + Total Debt vs FCF) both trace back to the same Total Debt figure and both are fundamentally about the debt load, but from genuinely different angles — near-term payment ability vs longer-term debt-service capacity — which is a normal split in real risk-factor writeups, not a restatement. Worth a second look if this recurs on other thin-coverage tickers, but not a fix for this eval.

## Issue found and fixed during this eval

First 1773.HK run failed `checkBulletGrounding` on one item: `example` computed "a 1% rise in borrowing costs on the $2.85B debt load ≈ $28.5M in annual interest" — arithmetically correct, but $28.5M isn't itself a FinancialFact row, so it read as a fabricated number. This exposed a real tension between risk.md's rule #3 (`example` must be a concrete scenario) and rule #2 (every number must trace to a fact): a concrete scenario naturally invites this kind of derived math.

Fixed by tightening `.claude/agents/analysis/risk.md` rule #2 to explicitly forbid computing new numbers in `example` — reuse exact fact-derived figures from `body`, or describe the marginal impact qualitatively ("would meaningfully increase interest expense") instead of computing a new figure. Re-ran 1773.HK after the fix: passed clean (see table above; both examples now qualitative where they'd previously computed a number).
